01 A little about me

Connecting the details
to the bigger picture.

My research focuses on computational modelling and optimisation of catalytic fluidised-bed reactors for dry reforming of methane—a route that converts methane and carbon dioxide into synthesis gas.

I combine computational particle fluid dynamics (CPFD/MP-PIC), reaction kinetics, data analytics and machine learning to connect particle-scale hydrodynamics with conversion, energy demand, catalyst-deactivation risk and process economics.

The aim is to develop reliable and efficient tools for low-carbon reactor design, scale-up and industrial decision-making.

03 Research notes

Questions behind the work.

Three threads running through my research.

ModellingHow do we connect particles to performance?+

I use CPFD/MP-PIC to investigate particle-scale hydrodynamics in catalytic fluidised-bed reactors. The question I return to is how those local behaviours connect to the outcomes we care about at reactor scale.

My work brings model validation and comparisons of reaction kinetics into that investigation, linking the flow description with conversion, energy demand and catalyst-deactivation risk.

OptimisationWhat should a better reactor optimise?+

In my research, reactor performance is a connected set of questions: conversion, energy demand, catalyst behaviour and process economics.

I explore AI-assisted techno-economic optimisation alongside reactor and regeneration strategies. The goal is to support decisions that account for these different dimensions together.

Scale-upHow can detailed simulations support decisions?+

One of my current projects explores automated reduction of CFD fields into chemical reactor networks. I am interested in how this connection can make detailed modelling useful for reactor design and scale-up.

It is part of a broader aim: building reliable and efficient computational tools for industrial decision-making.
